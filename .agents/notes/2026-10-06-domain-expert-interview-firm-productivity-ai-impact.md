# Domain expert interview: firm productivity and AI impact

Solo recorded interview (system audio Q&A). Miklos was the interviewee in a domain expert interview for an external platform. The session covered his research on firm productivity, executive talent, and the macroeconomic implications of AI.

## Research focus and approach

Miklos described his primary research area as firm competitiveness and productivity, using both theory and microdata. He links firm-level performance and production function estimation to aggregate economic development through macroeconomic equilibrium models.

Quote: "I'm interested in not only firm-level performance and estimating production functions, using panel datasets, but also how these firm-level studies can inform aggregate economic development in a macroeconomic model where things hang together and there are equilibrium constraints."

## Executive talent and firm performance

The most recent research studies managerial talent allocation and its effect on firm performance. The dataset covers 1 million Hungarian firms and 1.3 million executives. The approach tracks executive moves across firms and measures their impact on sales, profitability, and employment.

Quote: "We're treating the executive talent as unobserved. We don't have some observable variable that captures that, and we are trying to infer this from the performance of the company that they work for."

A key measurement challenge: only 50,000-60,000 CVs are available from a business almanac, far short of the full 1.3 million sample. The model separates three components: firm fixed factors, executive talent, and a match-specific residual. About 70% of performance variation is unexplained residual; 30% is attributed to firm and executive factors.

## Firm-level vs aggregate productivity

A central distinction: aggregate productivity can rise even without firm-level innovation, through reallocation toward more productive firms. Conversely, firm-level gains do not always signal economy-wide gains -- business stealing is a key counterexample.

Quote: "You can actually have economic growth even when none of the firms are improving. If there's a reallocation of resources towards the more productive firms, and so the good firms gain more resources while the bad firms are shrinking. On average, the productivity is going up."

## AI and small business implications

AI differs from past technological revolutions in requiring minimal complementary investment, making it accessible to very small firms. Small businesses may benefit relatively more than large ones by reducing fixed costs (admin, tax filing, translation).

Quote: "This might be one of the first technical revolutions where actually small businesses have maybe more to gain than big businesses."

## Policy recommendations

- Regulatory clarity, especially under EU AI regulation
- Informational campaigns in native languages
- Curated lists of regulator-approved AI tools
- Avoid locking in on specific vendors: competition and open innovation benefit small business consumers

Quote: "One thing I would definitely avoid is to really kind of lock in on one or two particular vendors. For a public policy campaign, that would be a big risk."
